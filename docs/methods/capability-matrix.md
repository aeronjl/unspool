# Capability matrix

**Supported** means the capability has a stable data contract, prospective scoring,
diagnostics, tests, and direct benchmark evidence. **Experimental** means it is usable but
its inferential or design coverage remains deliberately narrow.

| Scientific capability | Status | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical trial/session/animal representation | **Supported** | Identity, chronology, and source order are validated. |
| Task-level choice, omission, availability, reward, and response-time schema | **Experimental** | Validation is model-independent; categorical and explicit-omission likelihoods now consume the shared choice coordinate. |
| Labelled design matrices and fold-safe standardization | **Experimental** | Numeric, fixed-level categorical, interaction, explicit-reset lag/kernel, and training-only standardization terms are available. |
| Forward-session, within-session, and historical-cohort prospective validation | **Supported** | Learned preprocessing must still be fitted within folds; historical-cohort claims require the declared deployment order. |
| Complete-subject and complete-lab holdout | **Supported** | Does not by itself create population-of-labs inference. |
| Bias-only, psychometric, perseveration, lapse, and win-stay/lose-shift baselines | **Experimental** | Complete binary generative contracts; lapse support is fixed, and outcome-conditioned histories reset explicitly. |
| Parametric psychometric functions | **Experimental** | Five declared links with separate guess and lapse rates; thresholds are readable only against the declared link, stimulus scale, and threshold convention. `smooth()` and `hierarchical()` compose over the curve through its transformed coordinate; a rate the data pin to its bound is reported as a finding rather than shrunk. |
| Signal detection theory: yes/no, forced choice, unequal-variance ROC, and meta-d' | **Experimental** | Clean-room from the published equations; extreme-rate corrections must be declared rather than inferred, tables are pooled across subjects, and there is no hierarchical or smooth variant. |
| Temporal discounting and prospect theory | **Experimental** | Clean-room from the published equations, validated against the closed-form indifference point and the fourfold pattern; every coordinate is a logarithm, the amount unit must be declared rather than learned, and the inverse temperature's trade-off against the value function's curvature is reported as a `describe()` finding rather than fitted around. All three combinators compose over both -- `mix()` through the row coordinate, since these rows are independent -- and ambiguity is not implemented. |
| Normative belief updating: a Beta-Bernoulli ideal observer and the binary Hierarchical Gaussian Filter | **Experimental** | Clean-room from the published update equations; `pyhgf` is not wrapped, vendored, or consulted for numbers, and every convention published implementations disagree about is declared in the module docstring beside the equation it settles. Validated against four closed forms rather than against another implementation: the exact static Beta-Bernoulli posterior mean, the leaky observer's closed-form asymptotic learning rate, the constant-volatility Rescorla-Wagner reduction, and a zero coupling making a three-level filter's first two levels identical to a two-level filter's. The perceptual and response models are separate declared components, and both may declare rather than estimate the parameters most designs cannot locate. The third level's tonic volatility does **not** recover from binary responses on any reversal design tested; `belief_sensitivity()` measures that per study and reports it as a `describe()` finding before fitting. A negative third-level posterior precision is refused, never clipped, and a fit that succeeds reports the margin it succeeded by. All three combinators compose, with every row its own density block and the perceptual coordinate held constant within each subject/session block. |
| Scalar timing: duration reproduction and temporal bisection | **Experimental** | One lognormal scalar memory read two ways, so a clock rate and a Weber fraction from a continuous duration and from a binary report are the same coordinate and a study running both can ask whether one Weber fraction describes them. Validated against the exact coefficient-of-variation identity and against Church and Deluty's geometric-mean bisection point, with no fitting in either assertion. The bisection decision rule is **declared** and appears in the model name and signature: one anchor pair cannot separate the ratio and difference rules, which are a reparameterisation whose clock rates differ by exactly the ratio of their comparison durations, `(S+L)/(2*sqrt(S*L))`, so separating them is a comparison between models and no multi-pair model is offered. A central-tendency exponent is estimable from reproduction only. `smooth()` and `hierarchical()` compose over both, including over a within-session clock; `mix()` reaches bisection and not reproduction, because no shipped mixture component scores a bare duration. |
| Patch leaving as a threshold-crossing hazard | **Experimental** | Fits a giving-up intake rate and its multiplicative noise to residence times, with the hazard as the primitive and the density derived from it. Right censoring is declared per row and scored by its survival function; left truncation and interval censoring are refused rather than approximated. The marginal value theorem is a closed-form benchmark beside the model rather than the likelihood — `overstaying_ratio` reports the departure from it, which a model that fitted the theorem could not measure. A single-patch-type design cannot tell a rate threshold from a time threshold and says so before the fit. `CensoredDensityPrediction` retains the event-time density and exact survival probability at each observation limit, so its replayed score agrees with `pointwise_log_prob`. |
| Switching linear autoregression over a continuous behavioural series (`dynamax`) | **Experimental** | Optional wrapper. dynamax's E-step, M-step, filter, smoother, Viterbi decoder and sampler are wrapped and none is reimplemented; the ragged EM loop, the differentiated covariance, the exact-gradient stationarity check, the canonical state order and the Behavio parameter names are the wrapper's. One emission column only, because `DensityPrediction` tabulates one continuous coordinate. This is the first model in the package to declare `SMOOTHED` legitimately: the filtered one-step-ahead densities sum exactly to dynamax's own marginal log likelihood, and the smoothed path is a description of a recorded session that is never a score. `num_lags=0` is the nested Gaussian-HMM competitor, so the falsification is inside the wrapper rather than across packages. State order is canonicalised and its ambiguity and occupancy reported; canonicalisation does not make a label meaningful. |
| Static Bernoulli history GLM | **Supported** | Choice-only likelihood with declared covariates and lags. |
| Smooth longitudinal Bernoulli GLM | **Supported** | Fixed clock and knots; future knots are not learned from test data. |
| Hierarchical static and smooth GLMs | **Supported** | Gaussian partial pooling with population plug-in for unseen subjects. |
| Training-only nested candidate selection | **Supported** | Estimates the selection procedure, not one retrospectively named model. |
| Parameter and model recovery | **Supported** | Evidence is specific to the simulated design and parameter regime. |
| GLM-HMM | **Experimental** | Input-driven emissions support stationary transitions with an optional sticky self-transition prior or declared exogenous multinomial-logit transitions. Complete ILR transition regressions may partially pool by group under a relabelling-invariant Gaussian penalty. Dedicated single- and cross-subject session-dynamic classes implement Gaussian path priors and independent session transition rows shrunk to a global matrix, with path crossing and trajectory-recovery diagnostics. The initial distribution stays pooled, and state interpretation still requires alignment, recovery, and observable/smooth/learning competitors. |
| Binary Q-learning | **Experimental** | Compact two-action, session-reset implementation; `smooth()` and `hierarchical()` compose over it on the transformed coordinate, with the clock restricted to be constant within a reset block. |
| Composable binary RL | **Experimental** | Symmetric/asymmetric updates, unchosen forgetting, choice kernels, lapse-softmax policies, and explicit reset columns; `smooth()` and `hierarchical()` compose over the assembled agent per reset block, and rich assemblies require design-specific recovery. |
| Static and session-varying drift diffusion | **Experimental** | Joint choice/RT data with explicit units and fixed support assumptions; `smooth()` puts named parameters, a mixture weight included, on fixed knots. |
| Hierarchical drift diffusion, with or without smoothing | **Experimental** | `hierarchical()` composes over the static and the session-varying model alike; plug-in population prediction and bounded variance-component estimation. |
| Threshold learning landmarks | **Experimental** | Fold-fitted plug-in bootstrap; unresolved draws remain visible. |
| Cross-lab trajectory geometry | **Experimental** | Fixed empirical labs, no population-of-labs generalization. |
| Multinomial and omission-aware choice models | **Experimental** | Treatment-coded softmax regression supports finite JSON-scalar labels, trial availability, retained omissions, prospective scoring, recovery, and separate confidence, conditional top-label, and classwise reliability evidence; it composes with `smooth()` and `hierarchical()`. |
| Context-bound common fit artifacts and estimator registry | **Experimental** | Deterministic JSON binds task, data identity, version, parameters, and audits; it is intentionally not a lossless posterior format. |
| ArviZ/xarray posterior and predictive interchange | **Supported** | Immutable labelled groups preserve model, backend, dimension, and parameter-space provenance across both representations. |
| PyMC posterior inference | **Experimental** | NUTS covers the fixed-scale hierarchical Bernoulli history model and proper-prior binary Q-learning with labelled likelihood, predictive, and diagnostic groups. Q-learning additionally satisfies prospective sampled-estimator and full prior-joint SBC contracts. |
| A sampled candidate under a frozen study protocol | **Experimental** | `CandidateSpec.inference` declares it, declaration verification decides it against the supplied object, and the per-fold posterior convergence audit gates eligibility exactly as a failed numerical audit gates an optimized candidate. |
| Prospective comparison of two unlabelled-density candidates | **Experimental** | `ComparisonSpec.metrics` declares the scoring-rule set. A log-score-only comparison ranks continuous densities without fabricating a categorical Brier margin; unsupported declared rules are refused before fitting. |
| A prediction that can carry a right-censored observation | **Experimental** | `CensoredDensityPrediction` widens a continuous event-time density with the exact survival probability at each declared observation limit and requires the observed censoring indicator when replaying scores. Patch leaving is the first integrated family. Left and interval censoring remain unimplemented. |
| Latent session-varying GLM-HMM parameters | **Experimental** | `SessionDynamicBernoulliGLMHMM` fits one subject using the reference stationary → emission-dynamic/static-transition → fully dynamic MAP-EM sequence. `HierarchicalSessionDynamicBernoulliGLMHMM` adds a Gaussian population path and evolving subject deviations, one population label coordinate, direct population shrinkage of subject-session transition matrices, and separate seen-future/unseen-subject forecasts. Both retain path recovery and explicit transition non-smoothness. Dynamic local covariance, integrated unseen-subject prediction, and estimated path scales are not yet available. |
| Hierarchical lab effects | **Planned** | Requires more labs and a population-level sampling model. |
| Full Bayesian uncertainty propagation across the catalogue | **Planned** | Other first-party models still use local, bootstrap, or Monte Carlo approximations. |
| Neural-signal modelling | **Out of scope** | Neural observations should enter through explicit companion models or packages. |

A planned capability should not be approximated by forcing data through a supported method
with a different estimand.
